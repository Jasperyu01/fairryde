# Industry benchmark: how ride-hailing platforms other than Ryde define, adjudicate and automate rider-driver disputes

Compiled 5 Oct 2026 for the FairRyde team (Tencent Cloud AI CAN DO IT Hackathon, Ryde track). Ryde itself is out of scope (covered by another researcher).
Conventions: every claim carries a source link and the source date where known. "[older]" marks material dated before 2025. "[secondary]" marks a non-official source (news, blog, aggregator). Quotes are verbatim from the fetched page. Chinese sources keep the original term or a short quote with an English translation. Where a fetch tool returned a summary rather than the raw page, the wording here follows that summary; ambiguous items are flagged.

## 1. Singapore platforms (Grab, Gojek, TADA, CDG Zig): cancellation, no-show, waiting, fare, cleaning, safety and dispute rules

### Takeaway
Singapore's app platforms converge on a narrow band: a free-cancel window of 3 to 4 minutes after matching, S$3 to S$5 cancellation or no-show fees that are mostly passed to the driver, waiting fees of S$3 per 5-minute block after 3 to 4 free minutes, and arrival verified by GPS. For advance bookings Grab explicitly counts the driver's wait from the scheduled pickup time, not from early arrival. Cleaning reimbursement is capped (Grab S$120, TADA S$150) and requires a valid receipt. No Singapore platform publishes a numeric detour threshold: Grab's upfront fare excludes tolls and surcharges, and CDG charges S$5 per extra stop or extra 5 km and S$5 per pickup or drop-off change beyond 200 m.

### Cited Findings

#### Grab (Singapore)
- Grab's official cancellation policy page ("Fairer Cancellations for Everyone", effective date shown as 18 July 2022, still live when fetched 5 Oct 2026) sets a S$4 rider cancellation fee that is paid to the driver: "your driver will receive a $4 compensation from you" ([Grab SG cancellation policy](https://www.grab.com/sg/cancellationpolicy/)). [older, but current official page]
- From 18 July 2022 Grab cut both the free-cancellation window after a driver accepts and the free waiting period at pickup from 5 to 3 minutes; the cancellation fee is S$4 and the waiting charge S$3 per five minutes; services covered: "JustGrab, GrabCar, GrabCar Plus, GrabCar Premium, GrabPet and GrabFamily" ([Carsifu, 12 Jul 2022](https://www.carsifu.my/news/grab-users-in-singapore-to-pay-extra-if-they-keep-driver-waiting-for-over-three-minutes)). [older, secondary]
- Late-driver waiver: "Cancellation fees are waived if your driver does not arrive within 3 minutes after the first-shown estimated time of arrival" ([Grab SG cancellation policy](https://www.grab.com/sg/cancellationpolicy/)).
- Driver-initiated no-show fee: the driver may impose the cancellation fee only if the driver "Is at the pick-up location according to GPS data" and "Has waited more than 5 mins" ([Grab SG cancellation policy](https://www.grab.com/sg/cancellationpolicy/)).
- Other rules on the same page: "For GrabCoach, the cancellation fee is $10"; GrabHitch fees are waived "if your driver is still far away after waiting 5 minutes past the scheduled pick up time"; the policy excludes GrabExec, GrabExec 6, GrabAssist variants and GrabHitch; "Any fee incurred will be deducted directly from your Grab account. For passengers using cash, the fee will be added automatically to your next ride's fare"; wrongful charges are raised "via our in-app Help Centre" ([Grab SG cancellation policy](https://www.grab.com/sg/cancellationpolicy/)). No appeal window is stated.
- Waiting fee as reported in 2024: "grace waiting period of three minutes", then "a waiting fee of S$3 per five-minute waiting block" ([Mothership, 8 May 2024](https://mothership.sg/2024/05/grab-waiting-fees-confirm-pickup/)). [secondary]
- Precedent case (May 2024): a driver forgot to confirm pickup, so the rider's app showed the waiting fee rising (S$6 at 9:49am, S$9 by 9:53am) on a S$27.50 Jurong to Bras Basah ride. Grab attributed it to "the driver-partner's oversight", confirmed "no wait fee was charged", warned and retrained the driver, and said the app has "guardrails in place" to invalidate wait fees when it detects a mismatch between where buttons are pressed and the driver's actual position ([Mothership, 8 May 2024](https://mothership.sg/2024/05/grab-waiting-fees-confirm-pickup/)). [secondary]
- Fake or early "Arrived" taps: a rider complained in July 2022 that drivers tap "Arrived" before reaching the pickup point (in one case on entering a condominium compound), exposing riders to the S$3 per five-minute block charge; Grab had not responded at publication ([Mothership, 23 Jul 2022](https://mothership.sg/2022/07/grab-3-min-grace-period-complaint/)). [older, secondary]
- Advance Booking (official page, fetched Oct 2026): bookable "between 75 mins to 90 days ahead of time"; "Your driver will arrive early and wait for you for up to 15 minutes starting from the indicated pickup time"; to "Will I be charged a late fee if my driver arrives early?" the answer is "No, our drivers arrive early so that you can board the vehicle at the pick up time"; free cancellation "up to 1 hour before the start of the ride. However, the fare will not be refundable if you cancel within 1 hour"; the no-show fee "Will be chargeable only if you arrive more than 15 minutes after the scheduled pick up time"; if the driver is late "you will receive a voucher for your next ride" ([Grab SG Advance Booking](https://www.grab.com/sg/transport/advance-booking/)).
- When Advance Booking was revamped in May 2024 the lead time was "seven days to two hours", the driver wait was "extended to 15 minutes, up from the current three to five minutes", and riders were "charged the full fare for the trip if they cancel within an hour of the scheduled time" ([Mothership, 10 May 2024](https://mothership.sg/2024/05/grab-revamped-advance-booking/)). The booking window has since changed (see official page above). [older, secondary]
- Fares: "Know how much your trip will cost (excluding tolls and surcharges) before you book" ([GrabCar SG page](https://www.grab.com/sg/transport/car/)). Grab's Singapore Terms of Service (last modified 30 July 2026) define "Consumer Charges" to include "any applicable tolls, taxes and any other fees or charges that may be due for a particular use of the Service" (cl. 2.3) and say riders "may be charged a Cancellation Fee or such other Cancellation Fee as Grab may notify from time to time" (cl. 7.2.2); cleaning or damage fees appear in cl. 11 without published amounts ([Grab SG Terms of Service](https://www.grab.com/sg/terms-policies/transport-delivery-logistics/)). ERP is not named explicitly in the fetched extract.
- An unofficial c. 2019 fare guide lists S$5 per additional stop, S$3 per 5 minutes of waiting, and riders paying their own tolls ([Tunnel Time, c. 2019](https://www.tunneltime.io/en/singapore-info/grab)). [older, secondary, does not cite Grab]
- Cleaning: in a May 2026 case a driver claimed S$120, described as the maximum under Grab's policy, after a passenger vomited. Grab first rejected the claim as it was "unable to proceed with the claim without a valid receipt that meets the required criteria" (the receipt came from a friend's informal cleaning service), then credited it in full. Grab said it "offers a cleaning reimbursement fee for such incidents when the required documentation is provided" ([MustShareNews, May 2026](https://mustsharenews.com/grab-driver-reimbursement/)). [secondary]
- Safety (official page): "Grab continuously monitors the ride to detect unsafe scenarios and sends an alert to users"; optional audio recording "will be encrypted and only reviewed should a safety incident arise"; round-the-clock emergency assistance and in-app safety reporting ([Grab SG Safety](https://www.grab.com/sg/safety/)). No response-time commitment is published there.

#### Gojek (Singapore)
- Official rules effective "from 26 September 2022 onwards": a $4 fee applies if the rider cancels "After 4 minutes from when you've been matched to a driver" or "As soon as your driver arrives at your pick-up location", or if the driver cancels after waiting more than 4 minutes. Cancellation is free "Within 4 minutes from getting matched to a driver, as long as the driver hasn't arrived" and "If more than 5 minutes has passed from the initial indicated ETA, and the driver hasn't arrived" ([Gojek SG blog](https://www.gojek.com/sg/blog/news-cancellation-waiting-fee-gojek-singapore-2022)). [older, but current official page]
- Waiting fee: $3 once "Your driver's waited more than 4 minutes", then "Another $3 ($5 if you're on GoCar Premium) applies for every 5 additional minutes", capped at $9 ($13 Premium), which covers up to 19 minutes; "These waiting fees will fully go to Gojek drivers to compensate for their time"; vouchers cannot offset cancellation or waiting fees; GoTaxi has no waiting fee because the meter runs ([Gojek SG blog](https://www.gojek.com/sg/blog/news-cancellation-waiting-fee-gojek-singapore-2022)).
- Precedent case (Sept 2024): a rider was charged $4 after the driver cancelled following a 12-minute wait. Gojek's policy charges the fee "if a driver cancels the ride 4 minutes after arriving at the pick-up location". The rider later reported that "Gojek had refunded him after further investigation" ([The Independent SG, 4 Sep 2024](https://theindependent.sg/gojek-driver-cancelled-on-me-but-i-must-pay-him-4-customer-puzzled-as-to-why-hes-charged-when-the-driver-cancelled-the-ride/)). [secondary]

#### TADA (Singapore)
- From 18 March 2026 (12pm): "drivers will receive a S$3 compensation if a ride is cancelled after they have been travelling towards the pick-up point for at least three minutes, and are within about 500m"; previously compensation applied only after the driver arrived ([Mothership, 12 Mar 2026](https://mothership.sg/2026/03/tada-cancellation-cleaning-fee/)). The S$3 also applies if the rider cancels or fails to appear after the driver arrives ([AsiaOne, 11 Mar 2026](https://www.asiaone.com/singapore/ride-hailing-tada-new-cancellation-fee-cleaning-compensation)). [secondary; source is the NPHVA union's announcement]
- Cleaning fee compensation cap raised to S$150 to "better reflect" market rates; the previous cap and the evidence requirements are not disclosed ([Mothership, 12 Mar 2026](https://mothership.sg/2026/03/tada-cancellation-cleaning-fee/); [AsiaOne, 11 Mar 2026](https://www.asiaone.com/singapore/ride-hailing-tada-new-cancellation-fee-cleaning-compensation)).

#### ComfortDelGro (CDG Zig app)
- Official 2025 policy: cancellation fee after driver arrival "From $4.00"; no-show fee "From $5.00"; waiting fee $3 once "your driver has waited more than 4 minutes at the pick-up location", plus $3 per subsequent 5-minute block; up to 4 free cancellations a month after a driver is assigned (before arrival); the driver must wait more than 5 minutes before imposing a cancellation fee; location is verified by GPS. Cancellation and no-show fees apply "from 1 September 2025" and waiting fees "from 14 October 2025"; only Zig app bookings are covered (phone bookings exempt). Fee reviews go to "6552 4525" or "Main Menu > Account > Help Centre"; cash riders pay the fee on the next ride ([CDG cancellation policy 2025](https://www.cdgtaxi.com.sg/cancellation-policy-2025/)).
- CDG Zig Terms and Conditions (effective 6 Jan 2026) ([CDG Zig T&C PDF](https://www.cdgtaxi.com.sg/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/CDG-Zig-App-Platform-Terms-and-Conditions-of-Use-effective-6-Jan-2026.pdf)):
  - cl. 2.2: if the driver is "late for more than 10 minutes from the estimated time of arrival for an advance booking job, we will waive the prevailing advance booking fee" (except in inclement weather).
  - cl. 2.13: a "3-minute grace period is provided at pick-up locations". This conflicts with the 4-minute (waiting fee) and 5-minute (cancellation) thresholds on the policy page; it may apply to a specific product.
  - cl. 2.10: flat-fare jobs follow GPS-recommended fastest or shortest routes; for metered fares riders may request a route, otherwise drivers take the "most direct route".
  - cl. 2.17: an extra $5 "for every additional stop or distance traveled for 5km thereafter or less" (flat-fare bookings only).
  - cl. 2.21: "$5 per deviation" for rider-requested pickup or drop-off changes, except within 200 m on the same level; requires the rider's agreement.
  - cl. 2.14: "Any additional fees, including but not limited to entry fees, parking fees, ERP and toll charges etc., that are incurred either en-route to the pick-up location or en-route to the drop-off point, will be borne by the passenger".
  - cl. 2.7: CDG takes "responsibility for all payments ... This responsibility includes: refunds, chargebacks, cancellations and dispute resolution". cl. 3.1(c): no liability for loss or damage of riders' belongings.
  - Airport transfer schedule: no refund for "Passenger No-Show" after 60 minutes from flight arrival or 15 minutes at other locations; refunds for cancellation only "more than 24 hours before your pick-up time"; the operator may charge for "cleaning or repair" at its "sole and absolute discretion". No cleaning amounts are given for ordinary ride-hail.
- CDG taxi Advance Booking: "$8 advance booking fee"; bookable 30 minutes to 24 hours ahead; "No, waiting fee is not applicable as of now"; cancelling within one hour of pickup or a no-show costs "$10 / $12" (standard taxi) or "$20 / $22" (limousine taxi) (the page does not make clear which figure is which). If the driver marks "Arrived" early, riders are told to wait until the scheduled pickup time before cancelling, and fees "may be waived upon review" ([CDG Advance Booking](https://www.cdgtaxi.com.sg/advance-booking/)).

### Inferences
- The Ryde sample policy (5 min free wait, S$5 fee to the driver, 8 min no-show threshold) sits inside the Singapore fee range (S$3 to S$5), but its 8-minute no-show threshold is more rider-friendly than peers, whose drivers may cancel with a fee after more than 5 minutes (Grab, CDG) or more than 4 minutes (Gojek).
- For scheduled or advance bookings the Singapore precedent anchors the wait at the scheduled pickup time. Grab says so explicitly ("starting from the indicated pickup time"), and CDG tells riders to wait until the scheduled time. A rule base that starts the clock at early driver arrival departs from Singapore practice.
- Because Grab and Gojek quote upfront fares, a detour usually cannot raise the base fare. Singapore "overcharge" disputes are therefore more likely to involve add-ons (tolls or ERP, surcharges, stops, destination changes, waiting fees) or metered taxi bookings (CDG) than the base fare itself. This is an inference from the fare rules above, not a published statistic.
- Arrival verification is GPS-based everywhere, but no Singapore platform publishes an arrival geofence radius. The nearest published distances are TADA's "about 500m" (en-route compensation) and CDG's 200 m (deviation tolerance).

### Gaps
- The Seedly cross-platform comparison page returned no content (JavaScript-rendered), so it could not serve as a cross-check ([Seedly](https://blog.seedly.sg/waiting-time-and-cancellation-fee-charges-of-ride-hailing-services/)).
- Not found: Gojek's cleaning fee, Advance Booking wait rules and ERP or toll handling; TADA's waiting fees and evidence rules. A headline about TADA's earlier S$2.50 en-route cancellation fee could not be fetched (HTTP 429) and is undated ([Yahoo SG](https://sg.news.yahoo.com/tada-revises-cancellation-fee-policy-014031841.html)).
- Not found: Grab's explicit ERP treatment in upfront fares, Grab's cleaning receipt criteria and claim deadline, and who receives CDG's fees.
- No Singapore platform publishes an appeal window, a dispute SLA or a safety response-time commitment in the pages found.

## 2. Global platforms (Uber, Lyft, DiDi, Bolt): the same rule items

### Takeaway
Global platforms use the same skeleton. Western free-cancel windows are tighter (Lyft 30 seconds, Bolt 2 minutes, Uber US about 2 minutes, Uber HK 3 minutes), and wait time is billed per minute. DiDi has the most granular published changes: a 2-minute free cancel and 4 yuan fee (2022), 3 or 5 free waiting minutes then 1 yuan per minute capped at 5 yuan (2026), detour defined as driver-initiated departure from platform navigation (2025), and cleaning capped at 100 or 150 yuan (2022). For scheduled rides Bolt counts the wait only from the scheduled start, and Uber Reserve requires drivers to arrive early with wait included. On cleaning, Lyft publishes $25/$75/$150 tiers, while Uber decides case by case under strict evidence rules (device photo, receipt dated within 1 business day, claim within 3 calendar days).

### Cited Findings

#### Uber
- Hong Kong (official, undated page; no-show fee change effective 30 Jan 2024): no fee if the rider cancels before matching; a fee applies "if you cancel your trip 3 minutes after your request (or 6 minutes for Black trips)": UberX, Taxi, Assist and Pet HK$20, Comfort HK$25, UberXL HK$35, Black HK$40. A per-minute wait fee applies after the driver "has waited several minutes" (rate not stated). If the driver cancels after waiting, a "No-show Fee" applies. "Cancellation Fees pay driver-partners for the time and effort they spend getting to your location" ([Uber HK](https://www.uber.com/en-HK/blog/cancellation-fee-and-wait-time-fee)).
- US (secondary, 31 May 2026, says it checked official rate cards): free cancel "within 2 min of a driver accepting" (5 minutes for Black); wait grace "usually about 2 minutes after the driver arrives"; wait fee "roughly $0.30-$0.60 per minute"; cleaning fees approximately $20 (minor), $40 to $80 (moderate) and $100 to $150 (vomit or major liquid damage); disputes reviewed "against the trip's GPS log and any photo evidence" ([GetRideWise, May 2026](https://getridewise.com/blog/uber-fees-explained-booking-service-wait-cleaning)). [secondary]
- When an upfront fare changes (official help page, undated): "If your pickup point or destination change after you request the trip, your fare will be charged based on the time and distance of the actual trip"; "If your driver does not follow the estimated route and takes a detour, your fare may change to account for the extra distance driven"; "Heavy traffic may cause your trip to take longer than expected ... your fare may change"; "If your driver reaches your pickup location and waits for you for more than 5 minutes, you may be charged a wait time charge". Riders seek review via "I would like a refund" > "My driver took a poor route" ([Uber help: upfront fare not honored](https://help.uber.com/h/ff65490e-2ffb-41cf-a709-4611521c7b24)). The 5-minute figure differs from the US secondary source's "about 2 minutes"; thresholds vary by market and product.
- Tolls (ANZ, 2018): "The estimated toll component is replaced by any tolls that were actually incurred on the trip"; the fare also changes when stops are added or removed or the destination changes ([Uber ANZ blog, 6 Mar 2018](https://www.uber.com/en-AU/blog/upfront-fares-anz)). [older]
- Uber Reserve (Australia, 31 Jul 2023, updated 14 Feb 2025): the driver must "arrive 5 minutes before the pick up time" (15 for Black); "5 minutes of wait time is included in the fare" for standard trips (10 for Comfort, 15 for Black). "The rider will not be charged additionally for waiting time when you arrive early." Rider cancellation within 60 minutes of pickup or a no-show costs the lower of $25 or the upfront fare (UberX, Green, Pet, Assist), $30 (Comfort) or $60 (Black). Drivers must cancel at least 30 minutes before pickup (45 for Black), and more than 2 cancellations within 14 days removes access to Reserve requests ([Uber AU Reserve FAQ](https://www.uber.com/en-AU/blog/faqs-about-uber-reserve/)).
- Cleaning (official driver help, undated): "Cleaning Fee requests must be submitted within 3 calendar days of the trip"; evidence must include "A photo of the mess taken with the device associated with your Uber account" and a receipt with "the date, time, amount paid for the services, and name of the service provider or business", and "The receipt must also be dated no more than one business day after the trip". "Cleaning Fee payments are never guaranteed. Uber will review and decide all Cleaning Fee requests, including the amount granted, within Uber's sole discretion". No fees are paid for "unpleasant smells, cleaning supplies, time spent cleaning, or time spent not driving because of damage" ([Uber help: Rider made a mess](https://help.uber.com/h/6a60bda7-d8a6-46b7-bd95-2cdf62c7e987)).
- Cleaning (UK, 18 Jul 2019): "at least two photos of the mess"; "an official receipt, preferably stamped, with the date of the cleaning"; submit "within three business days"; "For Taxi drivers in London the maximum cleaning fee is £60 as prescribed by TfL"; quickly wiped messes such as spilt water usually do not qualify ([Uber UK blog](https://www.uber.com/gb/en/blog/uber-cleaning-fee/)). [older]
- Safety: Uber's US Safety Report covering 2021 to 2022 was "Published in August 2024"; incidents are reported to a "dedicated 24/7 Safety Support team" via in-app Help ([Uber US Safety Report](https://www.uber.com/us/en/about/reports/us-safety-report/)). The landing page gives no incident rates.

#### Lyft
- Rider policy (official): "You may be charged a cancellation fee if you cancel: More than 30 seconds after a driver accepts your ride". A no-show fee applies when the driver "Arrived at your pickup location", "Waited the required time or longer" and "Attempted to contact you (or you contacted them)". Fees "may vary based on how busy it is" (no amounts given). Scheduled rides incur a fee if "You cancel the ride within 1 hour of pick-up time, and a driver has been matched". Riders dispute through Ride history > "Dispute ride charge" ([Lyft help: rider cancel and no-show](https://help.lyft.com/hc/en-us/all/articles/115012922687-Cancel-and-no-show-policy-for-riders)).
- Driver side (official): the driver must "Wait for the time to count down to 0:00 without moving from the pickup location" and "Contact the passenger or respond if they contact you". "You won't get a no-show fee if you're: Too far from the pickup location" (no radius given). "Your account may be suspended if you have an excessive amount of no-show cancellations" ([Lyft help: driver cancel and no-show](https://help.lyft.com/hc/en-us/all/articles/115012922847-Cancellation-and-no-show-fee-policy-for-drivers)).
- Scheduled rides, driver side (official): go online "at least 30 minutes before the scheduled pickup time". At airports drivers "wait for up to 30 minutes for Standard (45 minutes for Black) which includes wait time pay"; at other pickups "an additional 2 minutes for Standard (5 minutes for Black)". "If you arrive before the pickup time, we'll notify the passenger in case they're ready to leave early". Drivers are paid when the passenger cancels within 1 hour if they are online and "not estimated to be more than 5 minutes late". Excessive cancellations reduce scheduled-ride access "for the next 14 days" ([Lyft help: scheduled rides for drivers](https://help.lyft.com/hc/en-us/all/articles/115012924387-Scheduled-rides-for-drivers)).
- Pricing (official): "Your final price may differ if: You wait more than 10 seconds before requesting the ride, You change destination or add stops, Trip takes longer or shorter than estimated, You add a tip". "Tolls: Covers any tolls encountered during your ride. Tolls are non-refundable." Airport fees are separate ([Lyft help: ride pricing](https://help.lyft.com/hc/en-us/all/articles/115012925707-Ride-pricing-and-charges)).
- Damage and cleaning tiers (official): $25 for "Minor damage such as small food/beverage spills, scuff marks, mud, dirt, or animal fur"; $75 for "Moderate damage such as biowaste outside of the car"; $150 for "Significant damage such as large biowaste spills that would require specialized cleaning". Riders get "an email ... and photos of the damage". Trash ("Food wrappers, empty water bottles, left behind trash") is not charged. To dispute, riders "reply directly to our follow-up email" ([Lyft help: damage fee](https://help.lyft.com/hc/en-us/all/articles/115012927367-I-was-charged-a-damage-fee)). No filing deadline is published.

#### DiDi (滴滴)
- Passenger and driver cancellation (Shanghai consumer test, 9 Feb 2022): "乘客可以在司机接单2分钟内免费取消" (passengers may cancel free within 2 minutes of the driver accepting); after 2 minutes a passenger cancellation costs 4 yuan, unless the driver is late or there is another platform-side cause; "到达上车点后一般等候时间为5分钟，超时后司机可以免责取消订单" (the usual wait at the pickup point is 5 minutes, after which the driver may cancel without liability) ([周到 民生测评, 9 Feb 2022](https://static.zhoudaosh.com/files/cnews/2022/20220209/821268961368B0331F67C19B7FB3C91BD3E5C54178AAFA7A27F669CF3E366E9B/5.html)). [older, secondary]
- Comparator in the same test, 享道出行: "乘客在司机接单后3分钟以内（含3分钟），可免责取消订单" (free cancel within 3 minutes of acceptance, inclusive); 5 yuan after that if the system predicts on-time arrival, free if it predicts a late driver; "等候3分钟后司机就可以免责取消订单" (the driver may cancel without liability after a 3-minute wait) ([周到, 9 Feb 2022](https://static.zhoudaosh.com/files/cnews/2022/20220209/821268961368B0331F67C19B7FB3C91BD3E5C54178AAFA7A27F669CF3E366E9B/5.html)). [older, secondary]
- Waiting fee (等待费), from February 2026: in tier-1 cities (Beijing, Shanghai, Shenzhen) "司机到达后免费等待3分钟" (3 free minutes after driver arrival); other cities keep 5 minutes; transport hubs get longer waits. "超时后按1元/分钟计费，单次封顶5元" (1 yuan per minute after the free period, capped at 5 yuan per trip); "超时1分钟即扣1元，不足1分钟按1分钟计" (partial minutes round up). The clock starts when the driver taps "已到达" (arrived); the whole fee goes to drivers; applies to 快车 and 特快 ([Sohu, Feb 2026](https://www.sohu.com/a/992204182_121504710)). [secondary]
- Trial version (31 Jan 2026): "1元/分钟，上限5分钟"; "如果乘客提前上车，费用按实际等待时长计算" (if the passenger boards early, only actual wait is billed); the driver keeps the waiting fee if the passenger then cancels, and after 5 minutes may cancel without penalty and still keep it; piloted in some cities ([NetEase, 31 Jan 2026](https://www.163.com/dy/article/KKJGTL9R0527BAIT.html)). [secondary]
- Reservation orders (预约单), pilot from April 2025: "司机接到预约单后，在接单后3分钟内取消，在取消订单算无责" (drivers may cancel a reservation within 3 minutes of accepting without liability); free waiting extended from 3 to 6 minutes; heavier penalties for cancelling "在出发前一小时内或者到达上车点后未等够免费等候时间" (within one hour before departure, or before completing the free wait at the pickup); empty-run compensation (空驶补偿) "一单最多可补偿80元" (up to 80 yuan per order), now computed as a share of the driver's estimated income ([Tencent News, 9 Apr 2025](https://news.qq.com/rain/a/20250409A038S600)). Further detail: if empty driving exceeds 1 km, "每增加 100 米可获 0.1 元额外补偿，最高 5 元" (0.1 yuan per extra 100 m, up to 5 yuan); pilot cities 台州、株洲、扬州 ([Sohu, 28 May 2025](https://www.sohu.com/a/899479887_120425551)). [secondary]
- Detour definition (June 2025): "司机主动协商变更路线并未按照平台导航指定路线行驶的视为绕路行为" (a driver who proactively negotiates a route change and does not follow the platform's navigation route is treated as detouring). Exempt: passenger-initiated changes, force majeure (closures, construction) and changes properly reported to the platform. Penalties: "扣除服务分、返还不当所得、支付信用违约金，甚至暂停服务、永久封号" (service-score deduction, return of improper gains, credit penalty, suspension, even permanent ban). The coverage gives no numeric distance or fare threshold ([Tencent News, 11 Jun 2025](https://news.qq.com/rain/a/20250611A03XWJ00)). [secondary]
- Machine fee-dispute review (费用异议, May 2016): DiDi described "interception review of all express and premier car orders" using route planning and trajectory data. It covered five problem types: charged with no ride, excessive bridge or parking fees, detours, meter started early, and meter running after drop-off. For confirmed detours DiDi "directly deducts erroneous fees" and the passenger pays only the estimated fare; launched in Beijing first ([快科技 MyDrivers, 20 May 2016](https://news.mydrivers.com/1/483/483331.htm)). [older, secondary]
- Cleaning fees (user service agreement, announced 8 Oct 2022): "舒适车型不超过100元" (comfort vehicles at most 100 yuan) and "商务车型不超过150元" (business vehicles at most 150 yuan), for "踩蹬车辆、呕吐、将食物或饮品洒落在车上等情况导致车辆脏乱、有异味" (treading on the vehicle, vomiting, or spilling food or drink so that the car is dirty or smells); "特殊情况以双方协商为准" (special cases by negotiation between the parties) ([Sohu, 8 Oct 2022](https://www.sohu.com/a/591033429_442726)). [older, secondary]
- Precedent (Chongqing, Nov to Dec 2024): a driver demanded 200 yuan after a carsick student vomited, mostly onto her own clothes, and cleaned up. DiDi said the fee is "司机与乘客协商而定，平台无权干涉" (settled by negotiation; the platform has no authority to intervene), called 200 yuan a "合理范围" (reasonable range) that includes "误工费" (lost income), and declined to reimburse. A lawyer said the driver should produce "相应清洗费用的票据" (receipts for the cleaning cost) ([Tencent News, 5 Dec 2024](https://news.qq.com/rain/a/20241205A02VNB00)). [older, secondary] This sits uneasily with the 100 or 150 yuan caps above.
- Malicious complaints (Sept 2019): DiDi targets passengers' "虚假投诉" (false complaints, for example claiming the driver never came, overcharged or took a wrong route) and drivers' "诱导取消" (inducing the passenger to cancel). Penalties: "根据情节严重程度停止服务3-30天" (3 to 30 days' suspension depending on severity); the first list named 36 passengers and 27 drivers. Evidence: "行车录音、车载视频、行程轨迹等多种方式" (trip audio, in-car video and trajectories) ([The Paper, 5 Sep 2019](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_4343741)). [older]
- Safety and conflict handling: see section 3 (DiDi safety AI 2026, conflict transparency report 2019).

#### Bolt
- Paid wait time (UK, official): the fee "starts after a driver has arrived at your pickup location and has been waiting for a certain period of time". Free waiting in London: Bolt, EV, Pet and Priority 2 minutes; Comfort 3; Premium, Luxury and Business 5; Assist 6; XL and XL+ 3. Other UK cities: Bolt, XL and Pet 3; Comfort 4; Premium 5. The fee accrues per minute "until the driver starts the journey" and "is only charged if the journey takes place and is completed". London drivers may cancel with a fee after 5 to 15 minutes depending on category ([Bolt UK wait time fees](https://bolt.eu/en-gb/support/articles/360019510579/)).
- Cancellation (official): "You won't be charged a cancellation fee if you cancel your request within 2 minutes from the moment driver accepted your request", nor if the driver cancels without having waited 3 to 8 minutes (varies by market). Disputes go to Support via the app; no review criteria are published ([Bolt cancellation fee article](https://bolt.eu/en/support/articles/360009457274/)).
- Scheduled rides (official, driver side): "The scheduled wait time of 5 minutes will be counted only from the scheduled start of the ride. In case you arrive earlier, the extra time spent waiting is not compensated." After 5 minutes from the scheduled start the driver taps Cancel and "the cancellation fee will be applied automatically"; a rider cancelling less than one hour before pickup is charged automatically ([Bolt scheduled rides](https://bolt.eu/en/support/articles/7769413257746/)).
- Driver guidelines (Latvia, official): cleaning compensation "covering 50% of the total amount, but not more than 50 EUR". The driver photographs the damage, obtains a car-wash receipt, photographs the receipt and submits it to Bolt. The page also states "after a customer has soiled your car, it is not possible to accept new orders". Drivers receive a cancellation fee if the customer cancels "more than 2 minutes after you have confirmed the order and have traveled most of the way" or "does not arrive at the call location within 5 minutes" of arrival. The fee "may not be charged if the time spent traveling to the customer exceeds the previously scheduled time", applies only to card payments and "is subject to Bolt's commission" ([Bolt LV driver guidelines](https://bolt.eu/en-lv/driver/guide/driver-info-guidelines/)).

### Inferences
- The global no-show test is a three-part conjunction: the driver was at the pickup (by GPS), the required wait elapsed, and contact was attempted. Lyft makes contact mandatory; other platforms rely on GPS and time only.
- Clock anchors differ by booking type. On-demand rides start the clock at driver arrival (Bolt, DiDi, Grab). Scheduled rides start it at the scheduled time: Bolt and Grab say so explicitly, and Uber Reserve's required early arrival with "no additional charge" for early waiting implies the same.
- A late driver forfeits the fee almost everywhere: Grab (ETA plus 3 minutes), Gojek (ETA plus 5 minutes), Bolt (travel longer than scheduled), DiDi (driver late), Lyft scheduled (more than 5 minutes late).
- Detour remedies are tied to the estimate or upfront fare (DiDi charges only the estimated fare; Uber adjusts "to account for the extra distance driven"), not to a published percentage threshold.

### Gaps
- No official Uber US page with current cancellation and no-show minutes and amounts was retrieved; US figures rely on one secondary source plus Uber's HK, AU and generic help pages.
- Uber publishes no cleaning fee tiers officially (case by case); the tier amounts above are secondary.
- Lyft does not publish cancellation or no-show fee amounts, or its required wait in minutes, on the pages fetched.
- DiDi's official rule center (滴滴平台规则 / 判责规则 pages) was not retrieved. All DiDi thresholds come from media coverage, not the rule text, and should ideally be checked against the in-app rule pages. No numeric DiDi detour threshold (km or %) was found.
- Bolt's cleaning rules outside Latvia and Bolt's route or fare-review rules were not found.

## 3. Automation and AI in dispute handling (DiDi, Uber, Lyft, Grab, Bolt; Chinese regulators)

### Takeaway
Published automation follows one pattern. Deterministic rules on structured trip data handle high-volume, low-value fee disputes: DiDi has screened all orders by machine since 2016, and Uber has auto-refunded cancellation fees by rule since 2017. ML or LLMs handle triage, agent assist and summarization (Uber COTA, Lyft with Claude). LLM agents run constrained by SOP trees with human handoff (Grab). Safety cases are always carved out to humans. Headline metrics: Lyft cut average resolution time by 87%; Uber's support redesign made resolution 55% faster (2017) and COTA over 10% faster (2018), worth "tens of millions" of dollars a year; Grab's SOP agents automate 87% of account-takeover investigations at over 99.8% accuracy; Grab's LLM partial-refund flow removed about 32,000 manual tickets a month. In China, regulators now demand evidence-based, explainable judgments with deadlines: Sichuan (Oct 2025) requires a 24-hour response and 3-day completion, and DiDi made case-by-case evidence checks (单单核实) mandatory in 33 cities in April 2026.

### Inferences
- The industry splits the work between rules and models. Fee eligibility (time windows, GPS presence, fare deltas) is decided by deterministic rules; ML or LLMs classify, gather and summarize evidence, draft explanations and route cases. FairRyde's Judge agent will be more credible if its verdict is anchored in an explicit rule evaluation, with the LLM explaining the rule outcome and handling the gray zone, than if the LLM decides freely.
- Every rider-facing deployment with published details routes safety to humans (Lyft, Grab missing items, DiDi safety AI with human experts). Mandatory safety escalation is industry standard, not an optional stretch goal.
- Grab's ">99.8%" accuracy and "false rate 0" are reported for internal investigation SOPs, not rider-driver adjudication. They should not be quoted as dispute-ruling accuracy benchmarks.
- China's regulatory trend (24-hour response and 3-day completion, hearing the driver, proportionality, evidence such as recordings and trajectories, multi-level appeals) is a ready-made external standard for "fair, transparent, explainable" rulings that FairRyde can cite.

- No Bolt engineering or product source on support automation; no Gojek or TADA automation sources.

## 4. Known failure modes and criticism, and the safeguards platforms added

### Takeaway
The documented failure modes are: fabricated or exaggerated cleaning claims (US "vomit fraud" in 2017 to 2018; a 2022 Lyft case with duplicated photos); fake arrival taps and GPS spoofing (Grab, Singapore); false rider complaints (over 35% of DiDi's in-car conflict complaints in Q1 2019); drivers inducing cancellations (DiDi's 诱导取消); incentive collusion with multiple accounts (Grab 2019); and wrongful automated sanctions against drivers (Amsterdam Court of Appeal 2023; Chinese drivers filing 41% of 2026 ride-hailing complaints, mostly over judgments). Safeguards seen: evidence-provenance rules (photo from the account's device, receipt dated within 1 business day, 3-day filing), GPS-consistency checks that void fees, selfie verification, published malicious-user penalties, recordings, and meaningful human review with transparency.

### Cited Findings
- Vomit fraud: drivers staged fake vomit and submitted photos to collect $80 to $150 cleaning fees; one Miami rider was charged $300 (two $150 fees in one night). Uber said it was "actively looking into reports where fraud may be detected and will take appropriate actions on those accounts" ([Vice, 23 Jul 2018](https://www.vice.com/en/article/uber-vomit-fraud-vgtrn/)). [older] The Miami Herald broke the story in July 2018. In October 2018 a North Dakota driver was charged after "purchasing food, throwing it on the inside and outside of his vehicle, taking photos of the alleged damage, then running the vehicle through a car wash" after dropping off the passenger ([Wikipedia: Vomit fraud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vomit_fraud)). [older, secondary]
- Retaliation and appeal path (Nov 2017): an Uber rider was charged $80 for a spill shortly after giving the driver one star over a longer route; Uber refunded him after investigation. A Lyft rider charged $150 for vomit had the fee upheld on time-stamped photos and recovered the money through a bank chargeback. Uber said it was "investing in additional resources to investigate cleaning fee claims" and can terminate drivers caught submitting fraudulent fees; Lyft said "fraudulent claims can result in immediate deactivation" ([CBS Sacramento, 6 Nov 2017](https://www.cbsnews.com/sacramento/news/call-kurtis-investigates-lyft-and-uber-riders-beware-of-cleaning-charges-well-after-you-reach-your-destination/)). [older]
- Weak photo evidence (2022): a rider was charged a $150 Lyft damage fee for cigarette burns on two low-resolution photos; "Two of them were identical" and they "didn't appear to show the same damage or even the same seat". Lyft rejected the appeals until a consumer journalist intervened, then refunded because "the description of the events did not warrant enough proof that the passenger created the damage" ([Elliott Advocacy, 15 Jul 2022](https://www.elliott.org/the-troubleshooter/whats-this-150-lyft-damage-fee-for-cigarette-burns-i-dont-even-smoke/)). [older]
- Evidence-provenance rules now in Uber's cleaning policy: a photo "taken with the device associated with your Uber account", a receipt "dated no more than one business day after the trip", and filing "within 3 calendar days" ([Uber help: Rider made a mess](https://help.uber.com/h/6a60bda7-d8a6-46b7-bd95-2cdf62c7e987)). Grab rejects claims without "a valid receipt that meets the required criteria" ([MustShareNews, May 2026](https://mustsharenews.com/grab-driver-reimbursement/)). Bolt requires photos of the damage and of the car-wash receipt ([Bolt LV driver guidelines](https://bolt.eu/en-lv/driver/guide/driver-info-guidelines/)).
- Robo-firing, Amsterdam Court of Appeal (April 2023), drivers against Uber and Ola: the court rejected Uber's "humans in the loop" defense, finding the human reviews "not… much more than a purely symbolic act", so the decisions were "solely automated" under GDPR Article 22. It rejected trade-secret arguments as "disproportionate compared to the negative effects of unexplained automated dismissals and the disciplining of workers" ([Fountain Court Chambers, 6 Apr 2023](https://fountaincourt.uk/2023/04/amsterdam-court-upholds-appeal-in-algorithmic-decision-making-test-case-drivers-v-uber-and-ola/)). [older]
- Follow-up (Oct 2023): Uber was ordered to pay €584,000 in accumulated penalties (€4,000 a day) for failing to give the ordered transparency on automated dismissals; it had again pointed to post-decision human review, which the court called "nothing more than a symbolic act" ([Worker Info Exchange, 5 Oct 2023](https://www.workerinfoexchange.org/post/uber-ordered-to-pay-584-000-for-failure-to-comply-with-court-order-in-robo-firing-case)). [older]
- GPS spoofing, fake apps and collusion (Singapore, May 2019): drivers used fake GPS on rooted phones, in one example to "plant their location in the middle of a shopping mall when they are actually far away"; tampered app versions allowed "unlimited cancellations of passengers"; fraudsters ran multiple accounts and phones to fake rides for incentive targets. Safeguards: periodic "selfie-verification"; Grab kept fraud below 1% and planned to grow its anti-fraud team from 120 to 200 staff ([Malay Mail, 18 May 2019](https://www.malaymail.com/news/world/2019/05/18/in-singapore-drivers-use-gps-spoofing-fake-apps-to-defraud-grab-says-ride-s/1754139)). [older]
- Fake or early arrival taps (Grab, 2022) and Grab's later stated guardrail that voids wait fees when button-press location and GPS position do not match ([Mothership, 23 Jul 2022](https://mothership.sg/2022/07/grab-3-min-grace-period-complaint/); [Mothership, 8 May 2024](https://mothership.sg/2024/05/grab-waiting-fees-confirm-pickup/)).
- False rider complaints and induced cancellations (DiDi): over 35% of 80,000 in-car conflict complaints in Q1 2019 were false ([Sina Finance, 6 Jul 2019](https://finance.sina.com.cn/roll/2019-07-06/doc-ihytcerm1671367.shtml)). DiDi publicly lists malicious passengers and drivers, covering false complaints and drivers' "诱导取消", with 3 to 30 day suspensions, using audio, video and trajectory evidence ([The Paper, 5 Sep 2019](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_4343741)). [older]
- Platforms declining to adjudicate: DiDi told a rider that a 200 yuan vomit cleaning fee was a matter for negotiation that the platform "无权干涉" (has no authority to intervene in) ([Tencent News, 5 Dec 2024](https://news.qq.com/rain/a/20241205A02VNB00)). [older]
- Driver-side backlash against opaque judgment: 41.13% of over 100,000 H1 2026 ride-hailing complaints came from drivers, mainly over judgments, deductions and suspensions ([NetEase, 2026](https://c.m.163.com/news/a/L335O6DH055394Y9.html)).

### Inferences
- Fraud detection must be symmetric. Rider-side abuse (false complaints, refund seeking) and driver-side abuse (staged cleaning claims, fake arrival, induced cancellations, no-show fee farming) both have documented precedents, and the Ryde sample policy sends the fee to the driver, which creates a driver-side incentive.
- Concrete red flags supported by the cases above: identical or duplicated photos; photos inconsistent with the vehicle or seat; a cleaning claim right after the rider gave a low rating; a mess "discovered" or staged after drop-off; several claims in one night; an arrival tap whose location does not match GPS; tampered-app or impossible GPS traces.
- Human review of adverse driver actions (deactivation, penalties) must be substantive. The Amsterdam ruling treats rubber-stamp review as no review at all, so a "human in the loop" in FairRyde should see the evidence and be able to change the outcome, with the reasons logged.

### Gaps
- The UK facial-recognition deactivation dispute involving Uber Eats (Pa Edrissa Manjang, reported settlement in 2024) could not be verified because the session's web-search budget ran out; it is not used here.
- No quantitative prevalence data for vomit fraud, GPS spoofing or no-show fee farming beyond Grab's "below 1%" overall fraud figure (2019) and DiDi's 35% false conflict complaints (2019).

## 5. Adjacent precedents for automated adjudication at scale (eBay/PayPal, Taobao, Airbnb, chargebacks)

### Takeaway
Large marketplaces show that most disputes can be resolved without humans when the process is staged and evidence-bound: eBay/PayPal handled over 60 million disputes a year with 90% resolved entirely by software (2010). Gray-zone cases can be crowd-judged quickly: Taobao used 31 anonymous jurors per case, decided by simple majority within 48 hours instead of 3 to 5 working days, and refined 140+ rules in its first year. Automated decisions keep legitimacy through fixed filing windows and an explicit right to human review: Airbnb sets 14 days to claim, 24 hours for the guest to respond, then Airbnb review, with an "option to request human review" of automated decisions.

### Cited Findings
- eBay/PayPal (Colin Rule presentation, 1 Nov 2010): "More than 60 million disputes per year", "90% resolved entirely by software". Flow: "FILE COMMUNICATE ESCALATE" (file, seller responds, buyer review, PayPal review, investigation complete). Design lessons listed: accessibility, automation, outcome versus time-to-resolution trade-offs, technology-assisted negotiation, participant satisfaction, quick enforcement, learning systems and user feedback ([Colin Rule, eBay/PayPal ODR, archives.gov PDF](https://www.archives.gov/files/ogis/events-presentations/acus-colin.pdf)). [older]
- Taobao 判定中心 and 大众评审, official launch Dec 2013: "每个任务都将由31位大众评审匿名通过投票来完成" (each task decided by 31 anonymous public reviewers); "获得票数多的一方胜出" (the side with more votes wins). Eligibility: an account registered 90+ days, Alipay real-name verification and a good credit record. Decisions "在48小时就做出判断" (within 48 hours) versus 3 to 5 working days before. Trial year: 820,000 reviewers, 340,000+ cases, 140+ rules refined ([Sina Tech, 31 Dec 2013](http://tech.sina.com.cn/i/2013-12-31/09369057168.shtml)). [older]
- Taobao in 2015: over 1 million reviewers had applied since Dec 2012, 30+ million dispute judgments, 5,000+ reviewers active daily, eligibility tightened to "在淘宝注册时间满一年" (account at least one year old), about 150 trading rules reviewed and corrected ([China News, 20 Jul 2015](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/m/cj/2015/07-20/7416799.shtml)). [older]
- Alibaba in 2019: "近636万人参与" (nearly 6.36 million volunteers). By Dec 2018 about 2.92 million people had judged 15.87 million disputes, with a "success rate" above 95% (definition not given). Case types: "消费者维权类、恶评鉴定类、市场共建类" (consumer rights, malicious-review identification, market co-building) ([21st Century Business Herald, 8 Jan 2019](https://m.21jingji.com/article/20190108/herald/544661e32766a79e48a097366a5f8ef1_zaker.html)). [older] The 2015 figure (30+ million) and the 2018 figure (15.87 million) are not comparable as stated; definitions (judgment tasks versus disputes) probably differ.
- Airbnb host damage protection (official): "Within 14 days of the responsible guest's checkout, file a reimbursement request in the Resolution Center"; evidence via "photos or videos, getting repair or cleaning estimates, and/or receipt"; "Your guest will have 24 hours to respond"; if the guest declines or does not respond, "Airbnb Support will then step in and review the request"; reimbursement "up to $3 million"; and "You will have the option to request human review and share more evidence or context" when disagreeing with an automated decision ([Airbnb Help: host damage protection](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/279)).
- Chargebacks as an external appeal: in the 2017 Lyft vomit-fee case the rider recovered the fee by disputing the charge with his bank after Lyft upheld it ([CBS Sacramento, 6 Nov 2017](https://www.cbsnews.com/sacramento/news/call-kurtis-investigates-lyft-and-uber-riders-beware-of-cleaning-charges-well-after-you-reach-your-destination/)). [older]

### Inferences
- Design lessons that transfer to FairRyde: (1) a staged flow of claim, counter-evidence window, automated ruling and human review on request; (2) fixed and short time limits (claim within N days, response within 24 hours); (3) evidence lists defined in advance per category; (4) an explicit, visible route to human review; (5) using hard cases to refine rules (Taobao's rule corrections, DiDi's public reviews), which maps onto the brief's "feedback loop from human overrides".
- A crowd or "public jury" mode (Taobao, DiDi 公众评议会) suits policy gray zones such as "should the driver wait longer in heavy rain?", not fact-finding on GPS logs. For a hackathon this is better shown as a precedent-library and policy-update loop than built as a real jury.

### Gaps
- Card-network chargeback automation rules (for example Visa's compelling-evidence standards) were not retrieved because the web-search budget ran out; no claims are made about them.
- No current (2024 to 2026) eBay or PayPal dispute volume or automation share was found; the 60 million and 90% figures date from 2010.

## 6. Business-case benchmarks (support cost, handling time, complaint incidence, churn)

### Takeaway
Hard ride-hailing support-cost figures are scarce. The best ride-hailing-specific numbers are: Uber's "Operations and support" expense of US$696M in Q2 2025 (about 5.5% of revenue, though this line is broader than customer support); a single late trip cutting a rider's later spending by 5 to 10% (Uber field experiment, 1.5 million riders); Lyft's 87% reduction in resolution time; Grab's 32,000 fewer manual tickets a month in one food flow; and DiDi's in-car conflict reports at 0.0042% of orders (2019). The most cited generic benchmark is Gartner's US$8.01 per assisted contact versus US$0.10 for self-service (2019).

### Inferences
- Computed from the 10-Q figures above: Uber's operations and support expense was about 5.5% of revenue in Q2 2025 (696/12,651) versus about 6.4% in Q2 2024 (682/10,700), so the line held roughly flat in dollars while revenue grew. This line includes more than customer support, so it is an upper-bound proxy, not a support-cost figure.
- Illustrative arithmetic on generic figures only: each dispute moved from an assisted contact (US$8.01) to self-service (US$0.10) saves about US$7.91, so 1,000 disputes a day would save about US$7,900 a day (about US$2.9M a year). This uses Gartner's 2019 generic averages, not Ryde data, and is an order-of-magnitude illustration.
- The churn evidence supports two design choices: fast, visible remedies, and explanations that avoid empty promises. In Uber's experiment a small coupon helped and promise-based apologies backfired.

### Gaps
- No reliable source was retrieved for average handling time per ride-hailing ticket, cost per ticket at Grab, Gojek, TADA or CDG, or the share of trips that generate a dispute at Western or Singapore platforms.
- No study was found that isolates the churn effect of dispute outcomes (for example a denied refund) as distinct from the bad trip itself.

## 7. Synthesis: decision factors and thresholds platforms actually use, by dispute category

### Takeaway
A defensible rule base can be built almost entirely from published parameters for no-show and cancellation (windows, waits, fees, late-driver waivers, scheduled-ride anchors, contact attempts) and for cleaning (tiers, caps, evidence, deadlines). Route deviation is the exception: no platform found publishes a numeric detour threshold. Platforms define detours by behavior (driver-initiated departure from platform navigation without rider request or force majeure) and remedy them by charging the estimated or upfront fare. Safety is handled by mandatory, fast human escalation rather than automated rulings.

### Inferences
- Decision factors for no-show (consensus): booking type (on-demand versus scheduled); wait anchor; elapsed wait; driver presence inside a pickup zone for the whole wait; contact attempts; driver lateness against ETA or scheduled time; rider location and responsiveness if available; history only as a secondary risk signal.
- Decision factors for route deviation: final fare versus upfront or estimated fare, split into components (base, tolls or ERP, surcharges, stops, destination change, waiting); actual versus platform-recommended route; who initiated the change (chat evidence); exogenous causes (closures, construction, heavy traffic); for metered bookings, distance versus most direct route.
- Decision factors for cleaning: evidence provenance (device, timestamp relative to trip end and to the next trip), receipt validity and date, severity tier, cap, filing deadline, exclusions, and fraud signals (duplicate photos, retaliation after a low rating, repeated claims).
- Decision factors for safety: always escalate; detect keywords and anomalies (route deviation, long stops, early trip end, sensitive conversation per DiDi); preserve evidence; give a human SLA rather than an automated verdict.

### Gaps
- Arrival geofence radius: no platform found publishes it. Nearest published distances: TADA "about 500m" (en-route compensation), CDG 200 m (deviation tolerance). The Ryde sample app log itself uses "within 10m" for auto-arrival (see the coordinator's DISP-002 note in this folder).
- Detour percentage or fare-difference thresholds: none published by any platform or regulator found.
- Event-logging tolerance (for example plus or minus 30 s on timers): no platform publishes one.

### Implications for the team's rule base and business case
Rule base (each item gives the evidence, then a recommendation; numeric choices without a cited source are the team's own policy parameters and should be labeled as such in the demo):
1. Make the wait anchor explicit and booking-type dependent. On-demand: anchor at verified driver arrival (Bolt, DiDi, Grab). Scheduled or advance: anchor at max(driver arrival, scheduled pickup time), following Grab ("starting from the indicated pickup time"), Bolt ("counted only from the scheduled start"), Uber Reserve (early arrival not chargeable) and CDG (wait until scheduled time). In DISP-002 this is decisive. If the booking is scheduled for 08:45, industry practice gives 6.0 minutes elapsed at cancellation, below the 8-minute threshold, so the fee would not be upheld. If it is on-demand with an ETA, the arrival anchor (8.0 minutes) is standard and the organizer's "upheld" label holds. Recommend modeling `booking_type`, encoding both branches, and asking organizers which applies; if "upheld" stands for a scheduled booking, treat it as a documented Ryde policy variant.
2. Use a three-part no-show test plus waivers: (a) driver inside the pickup zone by GPS for the whole wait, with minimum ping coverage; (b) elapsed wait at least the threshold from the correct anchor; (c) at least one contact attempt (Lyft-style). Waive the fee when the driver arrived later than ETA plus a grace period (Grab uses 3 minutes, Gojek 5) and when the arrival tap does not match GPS position (Grab's guardrail). Keep the fee amount, free wait and threshold as configurable parameters seeded from DISP-002 (S$5, 5 min, 8 min), and show the Singapore peer range (S$3 to S$5; 3 to 5 minutes) for context.
3. Route deviation: do not claim an industry percentage threshold; none is published. Define a transparent FairRyde parameter instead (for example: flag when actual distance exceeds the recommended route by more than X% and the fare impact exceeds S$Y), with exemptions mirroring DiDi and Sichuan (rider-requested change in chat or app, road closure or construction, rider-added stops or destination change). Default remedy: refund the difference down to the upfront or estimated fare (DiDi's 2016 precedent). Penalize the driver only after evidence review and proportionately (Sichuan). Under Singapore upfront fares, first check whether the "overcharge" is really tolls or ERP, surcharges, stops or waiting fees (Grab excludes tolls and surcharges from the upfront fare; CDG passes ERP and tolls to the rider and charges S$5 per extra stop, extra 5 km or pickup change beyond 200 m).
4. Cleaning (stretch): a three-tier schedule modeled on Lyft ($25/$75/$150) and scaled to Singapore caps (Grab S$120, TADA S$150). Require at least 2 photos from the driver's account device timestamped after drop-off and before the next trip, a receipt dated within 1 business day, and a claim within 3 calendar days (Uber). Exclude smell-only, trash and water spills (Uber, Lyft).
