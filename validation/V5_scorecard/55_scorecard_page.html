How the run went

125 minutes of wall time against a 60-minute plan. Two agent steps set the pace: writing the 15 cases (48 min) and applying the KB to 11 of them (33 min). Every primitive wrote a start and an end line to run_log.jsonl; tools/timeline.py turns that log into this chart.

Limits of this validation

  • Samples are small, so the intervals are wide. The decision rests on repeated CRITICAL patterns more than on any single rate.
  • The end-to-end references come from two AI agents that read only Ryde's texts. They agreed on all 15, which also suggests the cases were cleaner than real disputes. A person should check the four mismatches.
  • On the two rules both reviewers saw, they disagreed once (CW-F04), so single-reviewer verdicts carry noise.
  • CLS-03 passed the rule review, but the taxonomy layer exposed its safety gap (C08). Counting it, M3 would be 12 of 20.
  • A strong model applied the KB with plenty of time. The production agent may apply it less faithfully; that is a separate test.
  • The builder pre-registered the plan. Its hash was recorded before any result and is unchanged.

Re-validation after the fixes

  • doubled samples (pre-registered expand rule): taxonomy 40 new texts, ontology 28, rules 40 including all 17 B and C rules as a census, end-to-end 30
  • new seed and new texts and cases, because the fixer has seen this round's samples
  • M5 is computed on the 15 new held-out cases only; the 15 old cases become a regression check
  • two reviewers on a quarter of the rules (this round they disagreed on 1 of 2 shared rules)
  • a human (Jasper or a teammate) spot-checks every end-to-end mismatch
  • Same verdict definitions, metrics and thresholds; the plan is hashed again before results.
